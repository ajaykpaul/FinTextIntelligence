OSACT5 label audit: how to annotate
===================================

This measures how often OSACT5's offensive / not-offensive labels are wrong. It needs
one person who reads Gulf and Egyptian Arabic comfortably. A second, independent
annotator makes the result much stronger, so use two if you can, each with their own
copy of the sheet.

The files
---------

    data/interim/label_audit/osact5/sheet.csv   ← give this to the annotator
    data/interim/label_audit/osact5/key.json    ← the original labels; don't open it until scoring

Both are under data/, which is never zipped or uploaded: the tweets are covered by the
shared task's research terms.

sheet.csv has 200 tweets in random order. Half were labelled offensive by the original
annotators and half were not, but nothing in the sheet says which. Open it in Google
Sheets or Excel (it's UTF-8, so the Arabic shows correctly).

What to fill in
---------------

  column      what to write
  ----------- -------------------------------------------------------------
  offensive   1 if the tweet is offensive, 0 if it isn't
  unsure      1 if you genuinely can't decide; leave offensive empty then
  note        optional: a word on why, for anything borderline

Leave item and text alone. Rows marked unsure are counted but kept out of the rate.

What counts as offensive
------------------------

Use the shared task's own definition, not your own sense of what's acceptable. The point
is to check whether the labels follow the dataset's rules, so the rules have to be the
same ones.

A tweet is offensive if it contains any of:

- an insult or attack aimed at a person or group, explicit or implied (name-calling,
  "dog", "donkey", mocking someone's looks or intelligence)
- vulgar or obscene language, including profanity used for emphasis
- a curse or wish for harm aimed at someone ("الله ياخذك")
- hate speech: attacks based on religion, race, ethnicity, gender, nationality, social
  class or disability

A tweet is not offensive if it is:

- criticism or disagreement without insult ("this decision is wrong")
- strong emotion with no target ("I'm so angry today")
- an emoji alone, even 😡 or 👊, unless the words make it an attack
- reporting or quoting offensive language in order to condemn it

Placeholders like @USER, URL and <LF> were added by the dataset; ignore them.

Go with your first reading. Don't look tweets up, don't search for context, and don't go
back to change earlier answers after seeing later ones. Two hundred tweets take about an
hour and a half.

Scoring
-------

From the project folder:

    PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m fintext.cli label-audit score data/interim/label_audit/osact5/sheet.csv

With two annotators, score each sheet separately. It prints the agreement with the
original labels, Cohen's kappa, the two kinds of flip with 95% intervals, and the
estimated noise rate for the whole corpus (reweighted from the 50/50 sample to OSACT5's
real 35.5% offensive).
